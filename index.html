Breast Cancer
by the Numbers.

Survival When Found Early

99%

According to the American Cancer Society, the 5-year relative survival rate for breast cancer diagnosed at an early, localized stage is over 99%.

Source: American Cancer Society

Survival, All Stages

92%

Overall, 92% of female breast cancer patients had not died from their cancer 5 years later. Survival rates differ depending on the stage at which the cancer was detected.

Source: CDC

Linked to Inherited Genes

1 in 10

Inherited gene changes are passed on in families and are present in cells throughout the body. About 1 in 10 breast cancers are linked to inherited gene changes.

Source: American Cancer Society

Recommended Screening Ages · U.S.

40–74

U.S. experts recommend a breast cancer screening every two years from age 40 to 74.

Source: USPSTF

New Invasive Cases · U.S. 2026

322K

About 321,910 new cases of invasive breast cancer will be diagnosed in women in the U.S. in 2026.

Source: American Cancer Society

Know the facts

5 Facts Every Woman
Should Know.

  1. Early Detection Saves Lives.Mortality drops when breast cancer is found and treated early.
  2. Small Lumps Are Easier to Treat.Tumors that haven't reached the lymph nodes are more likely to be treated successfully.
  3. Know When to Get Checked.The recommended age to start screening varies by country and personal history. Ask your doctor when you should start.
  4. Men Can Get It Too.About 0.5% to 1% of breast cancers occur in men.
  5. Progress Is Real.In high-income countries, breast cancer mortality fell 40% between the 1980s and 2020.

Source: World Health Organization.

FAQ

Frequently Asked
Questions.

What is Rise in Pink?

Rise in Pink is an independent initiative led by Unicoin Foundation to raise breast cancer awareness and encourage our community to support early detection, education and patient support.

Who is leading the campaign?

Rise in Pink is independently organized by Unicoin Foundation. Rise in Pink and Unicoin Foundation are not affiliated with, sponsored by or endorsed by National Breast Cancer Foundation (NBCF).

Where does my donation go?

Donations are made directly through NBCF’s donation platform. Neither Rise in Pink nor Unicoin Foundation receives or administers these funds. NBCF manages donations according to its own policies and charitable programs.

How do I receive unicoins for my donation?

The Unicoin Foundation team will contact you to request proof of your transfer to NBCF and the information needed to complete the necessary due diligence before issuing your unicoin certificate. You will receive one unicoin for every eligible US$1 donated, while supplies last and until the 100,000-unicoin allocation has been exhausted.

What does “One dollar donated. One unicoin received.” mean?

The proposed recognition program provides one unicoin for each eligible US$1 donated, while the campaign’s allocation lasts. This describes the number of tokens offered, not their market value. It does not mean that one unicoin is worth US$1 or promise any financial return.

Is my donation a purchase of unicoins?

Donations are made directly through NBCF’s donation platform to support its charitable work. Any unicoin recognition would be provided separately by Unicoin Foundation, not sold or issued by NBCF. The recognition program is independent of NBCF and does not imply its affiliation, sponsorship or endorsement.

How many unicoins are available for this campaign?

Unicoin Foundation has designated a maximum allocation of 100,000 unicoins for the proposed Rise in Pink recognition program. Availability would end when that allocation is exhausted.

What kind of support can my donation help provide?

NBCF’s programs include mammograms and diagnostic services for women in need, patient navigation, breast health education, support groups and HOPE Kits® for people undergoing breast cancer treatment. Donations support NBCF’s work according to its own funding priorities; Rise in Pink does not determine how individual donations are allocated.

How can I help besides donating?

Share Rise in Pink with your community using #RiseInPink, share trusted breast health resources, and encourage those around you to speak with a healthcare professional about appropriate screening. You can also explore volunteering and other ways to support the cause through NBCF’s official website.
